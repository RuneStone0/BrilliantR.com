Case study

Recovering a hacked e-commerce website

The situation. A small business owner reached out after discovering their long-running online store had been hijacked. Their website — representing years of built-up search engine credibility — had been silently replaced by a gambling operation. Customers searching for their products were landing on an unrelated gambling site, and the malicious content had begun appearing in Google results under their domain name.

The challenge. This wasn't a simple hack to undo. The attacker had gained deep access to the hosting infrastructure, giving them control over both the website and the domain's DNS routing. That made recovery more complex than just cleaning infected files — we had to rebuild trust in the domain itself before we could restore the site.

What we did

  • Website recovery — identified the attack vector, moved the site to clean infrastructure, and restored the storefront and all e-commerce functionality.
  • Domain & SSL — redirected DNS through a secure registrar path and provisioned a fresh SSL certificate.
  • Email recovery — restored staff email accounts that had been disrupted during the infrastructure change.
  • Stability — diagnosed and resolved a follow-on server overload issue that surfaced after the restoration.

The result. The client's store was back online and processing orders. Staff email was working. And for the first time, the site had proper security tooling in place to catch threats early — security monitoring, fully updated software, and two-factor authentication on every account.
